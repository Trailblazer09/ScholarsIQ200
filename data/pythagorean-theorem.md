# The Pythagorean Theorem

## Statement
The Pythagorean theorem is a fundamental relation in Euclidean geometry among the three sides of a right-angled triangle. It states that the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. It is written as a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse and a and b are the two legs.

## How to Use It
To find the hypotenuse when you know the two legs, square each leg, add them together, and take the square root. For example, with legs of length 3 and 4: 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25, and the square root of 25 is 5, so the hypotenuse is 5. To find a missing leg, rearrange the formula: a² = c² − b².

## Pythagorean Triples
A Pythagorean triple is a set of three positive integers that satisfy the theorem. Common examples include (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), and (8, 15, 17). Any multiple of a triple is also a triple, such as (6, 8, 10). These are useful for quickly recognising right triangles with whole-number sides.

## Real-World Applications
The theorem is used in construction, navigation, and computer graphics. Builders use the 3-4-5 rule to ensure corners are square. It also underpins the distance formula in coordinate geometry, which calculates the straight-line distance between two points as the square root of the sum of the squared differences in their coordinates.
