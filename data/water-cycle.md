# The Water Cycle

## Overview
The water cycle, also called the hydrologic cycle, describes the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. Water changes between liquid, vapour, and solid states as it circulates. The cycle is driven by energy from the Sun and the force of gravity, and it has no real beginning or end.

## Evaporation and Transpiration
Evaporation occurs when the Sun heats water in oceans, lakes, and rivers, turning it into water vapour that rises into the atmosphere. Transpiration is a related process in which plants release water vapour through tiny pores in their leaves. Together these processes, sometimes called evapotranspiration, move large amounts of water into the air.

## Condensation and Cloud Formation
As water vapour rises, it cools and condenses into tiny droplets, forming clouds and fog. Condensation is the process of water vapour turning back into liquid. The droplets gather around tiny particles in the air such as dust. When enough droplets combine, they grow large and heavy.

## Precipitation and Collection
When water droplets in clouds become too heavy to stay aloft, they fall as precipitation: rain, snow, sleet, or hail, depending on temperature. This water collects in oceans, rivers, and lakes, or soaks into the ground to become groundwater. Surface runoff and groundwater eventually return water to the oceans, and the cycle repeats.

## Importance
The water cycle distributes fresh water across the planet, supports all living things, shapes weather and climate, and replenishes the rivers and aquifers that people rely on for drinking water and agriculture.
